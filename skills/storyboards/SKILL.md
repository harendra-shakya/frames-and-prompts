# Storyboarding and sequential storytelling

A storyboard is a **visual argument about what happens next**. Gorgeous isolated frames that do not communicate a story are not a usable storyboard.

## Derive shots from dramatic beats

Before drawing, write:

- **Want:** what does the character try to do?
- **Obstacle:** what prevents it?
- **Change:** what new fact, emotion, or action alters the situation?
- **Payoff:** what must the viewer understand or feel after the scene?

Map those beats into shots or panels. Every cut should reveal new information, change scale/perspective, accelerate/decelerate time, or show a meaningful reaction.

## Build a production shot/panel sheet

For each numbered beat include:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Story purpose | New information or emotional shift |
| Subject/action | Who does exactly what |
| Composition | Shot size, angle, subject location, focal hierarchy |
| Geography | Camera axis, movement/screen direction, eyeline, entrances/exits |
| Continuity | Costume, props, positions, environment markers, time/light |
| Time | Shot duration or comic panel dwell/reading rhythm |
| Motion/audio | Action cue, speech/VO, sound effect, silence when meaningful |
| Transition | Why the next panel or shot follows |

For comics, account for **reading order, gutter inference, lettering space, panel shape, and what happens between panels**. For film, account for blocking, screen direction, duration, cuts, and sound bridges.

## Reference correctly

- [ShotDeck](https://shotdeck.com/), [Flim](https://flim.ai/), [Frame Set](https://frameset.app/search): composition, lighting, coverage and shot treatment.
- [Sakugabooru](https://www.sakugabooru.com/): timing, action staging and expressive key poses.
- [Art of the Title](https://www.artofthetitle.com/): title/sequence structure.
- [Futurepedia AI Style Atlas](https://www.futurepedia.io/resources/ai-style-atlas/view): style research, **not** evidence of narrative clarity.

Annotate the specific technique that you are borrowing from each reference.

## Validate before final rendering

Do a **thumbnail pass** first. Can the scene be understood from small grey boxes with silhouettes and arrows? If not, change staging, not rendering detail.

Then verify continuity across adjacent shots: same left/right relationships, prop ownership, cast identity, geography, weather, palette, and lighting unless a motivated transition changes them. Make a character/location reference sheet when repeating them.

Make an animatic for timing-sensitive film or action sequences; a contact sheet for static comics. Check the whole sequence, not just the best frame.

**Reject when:** characters teleport, action reverses screen direction accidentally, dialogue has no visual reaction, beats are missing, panel order is ambiguous, each panel uses the same camera angle, or an impressive frame interrupts narrative comprehension.

**Deliver:** numbered shot/panel plan, thumbnails/contact sheet or animatic, stable reference notes, and continuity issues discovered.
